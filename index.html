THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM

Access ≠ capability.

Electronic markets, mobile brokers and APIs have made sophisticated instruments broadly accessible. But the infrastructure required to understand, govern and prove financial decisions remains fragmented and asymmetric.

WHAT HAS BEEN DEMOCRATIZED

Market access

  • Low-friction brokerage
  • Futures and options access
  • Real-time market interfaces
  • APIs and programmable execution
  • Mobile-first participation
WHAT HAS NOT

Trustworthy market intelligence infrastructure

  • Causal market-state reconstruction
  • Data and model lineage
  • Decision provenance
  • Risk and capital authority
  • Execution truth and reconciliation
  • Deterministic replay and evidence
The next phase of financial democratization is not simply cheaper access to markets. It is access to trustworthy financial intelligence infrastructure.
CATEGORY DEFINITION

Intelligent Financial Market Provenance Infrastructure

Infrastructure that maintains a verifiable chain from market reality to financial action.

TORAM CATEGORY THESIS

Financial provenance is more than an audit log. It means being able to establish what the system knew, where that knowledge came from, how it was interpreted, why action was permitted, what was executed, and what happened next.

01

Market provenance

Source, instrument, timestamp, depth, sequence and data integrity.

02

Intelligence provenance

Feature, model, rule, configuration and revision that interpreted the market.

03

Decision provenance

ENTER, HOLD, EXIT or NO TRADE — and the state that caused the decision.

04

Authority provenance

Risk, capital, permission, session and policy boundaries that allowed or blocked action.

05

Execution provenance

Intent, order, broker acknowledgement, fill, reconciliation and ambiguity handling.

06

Outcome provenance

Market response, slippage, position result, failure state and deterministic replay.

WHY THIS CATEGORY EMERGES NOW

AI makes financial reasoning more accessible. It does not automatically make financial action trustworthy.

As models, software agents and automated systems gain the ability to reason about markets, finance needs an infrastructure layer that can answer a harder question than “what did the AI say?”

What information was actually available at that moment?
Which intelligence revision made the decision?
Was the system permitted to put capital at risk?
What did the broker actually execute?
Can the full decision be reconstructed later?
Intelligence may be probabilistic.
→
Financial authority must be provable.
